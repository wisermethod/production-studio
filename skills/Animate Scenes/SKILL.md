# Animate Scenes

## Context

Writes no script, narrates nothing, synthesizes no speech, captures nothing, cuts no recording, joins no scenes and sets no loudness. For an avatar scene or window, it uploads the take's narration to HeyGen and requests a paid render; it publishes nothing. It draws no explainer's picture: `skills/Animated Explainer/` and `skills/Illustrator/` in `wiser` make it. Use for the caller's made scenes: walkthroughs animate one product still through `tools/Scene Composer/` here and `tools/video-render/` in `wiser`; explainers run Animated Explainer by name; and, with a HeyGen account, avatar scenes show the member's selected avatar speaking the scene's current take. A walkthrough or explainer may instead carry an avatar window at the bottom right for a stretch of its narration, added after the base renders and before review. A window is a presentation property, never another scene kind or take.

The avatar path sends HeyGen a prepared upload of that take's narration and a render request. Upload retains narration at HeyGen and has no independent fee established; renders spend the member's separate API-wallet dollars by rendered second, including padding or guards, at the selected look type's rate. The Cost step discloses that rate and the two-render worst case before billing. Never send `script` with a `voice_id`, use HeyGen's own speech or replace the take, even when the look is public.

Avatars are optional, offered once as available with a HeyGen account and never pressed. Without a connected `heygen` connector, make every scene that does not depend on it; a requested window can retain its rendered base while the window waits. A missing or unready connection takes Gateway answers below and **Connect Account** in its own human turn; never make the rest of a production wait for an account it does not need. An avatar stop or pending answer holds only its dependent work: record it and finish the other supplied scenes before returning that question or handoff.

Selecting an existing avatar asks no consent question and takes no sample. Under this plugin's Standing constraint, cloning a real person's voice clone or likeness asks once, before any sample, trusts the member's yes without proof and reuses a saved yes across productions. This skill clones neither. A request to make a new likeness from a photo, footage or webcam goes to **Set Up Voice Clone**'s declared gap, **Cloning a person's likeness**, before any sample is opened or sent; a saved yes does not close that gap. Member-supplied music and footage are treated as cleared.

All walkthrough labels are the script's checked `On-screen words`, chips are supplied text, and captions are the words narrated. This skill writes no prose. Captions in a written form different from the words narrated, an address or a symbol say, are missing here; report that gap rather than transcribing them into a different form. Animated Explainer binds its own memory keys. This skill's `design` is optional. Drawn captions over an avatar scene are also missing: stop that scene before upload when requested, name the gap and continue independent scenes.

Classifier seam: none.

## Objective

Make the supplied scenes in order, with one production frame, lead and tail, and write `<production>/scenes/scenes.json` for the assembling step. Keep a ready scene whose inputs have not changed without touching its files or running its tools. A new scene becomes ready only under an Animator ship verdict covering its key. Verify against Success.

## Inputs

Never choose a different filed take.
- `<scenes>`: the made scenes in order, supplied by the caller or the member, never inferred from the script. Each has its scene id and `kind`, `walkthrough`, `explainer` or `avatar`. That object, or any other shape without the Cost step's post-take avatar scope, covers narration alone and is never avatar coverage. Read `<production>/scenes/avatar-approval.json` for saved avatar coverage; a supplied approval with that post-take scope can be filed there by the Cost step. No second cost question for covered work.
- `<target>`: frame width, height and rate; caption mode, `none` or `drawn`, with the complete drawn style Scene Composer declares; any chip text by scene; and lead and tail where they differ from that tool's defaults.
- `<design>`: the optional `design` binding, resolved per Workspace Model in `AGENTS.md` in `wiser`, supplying colours by role and local font files. Available: use the roles it names. Unbound or unavailable: report the default palette and proceed. Does a scene draw text, a callout label, a chip or drawn captions, without a font file? Yes: use the font already recorded for this production in its scene inputs; none recorded: ask the member for one, once for the production, and wait. Record its path in `scenes.json` and its declaration and hash in `inputs.json`, as step 2 says, and use that choice for every scene. No: use the supplied fonts where needed. A file the member hands over is treated as cleared.

## Scene record

`<production>/scenes/avatar-approval.json` is the one production-scoped avatar cost record. It holds the member's `amount` in USD, exact `words`, `date`, estimate, itemized scope and billing basis from the Cost step, including approved extensions. Each avatar scene and window reads it before upload; a narration-only caller approval is never saved as avatar coverage. Read it before writing; changed bytes since the last read stop the write for reconciliation. A failed save stops uncovered avatar work before upload.

`<production>/scenes/scenes.json` is a UTF-8 JSON object with these keys, which the assembling step reads by name:

- `frame`: `width`, `height` and `fps`, the production's target.
- `lead` and `tail`: the resolved production values, in seconds, shared by every scene so the assembling step can start each scene's narration at `lead`.
- `order`: the caller's scene ids in order.
- `scenes`: an object mapping each scene id to these keys:
  - `kind`: `walkthrough`, `explainer` or `avatar`.
  - `key`: step 2's full SHA-256, or `null` if a stop prevented its computation.
  - `folder`: the scene folder relative to the production, or `null` before one exists.
  - `inputs`: `record`, the `inputs.json` path relative to the production and its `sha256`; `script_sha256`; `take`, its `cid` and record `sha256`; `still_sha256` for a walkthrough or `sources` with each source's identity and `sha256` for an explainer; and `fonts`, each declaration's source path and `sha256`. Use `record: null` before filing and retain only hashes actually measured.
  - `outputs`: each existing output as an object with `path` relative to the production and `sha256`. Names are `timing_specification`, `timing`, `captions`, and, for a walkthrough, `specification`, `composition` and `render_record`; an explainer instead has `delivery_record`. A finished render adds `segment` and `frame_hashes`. `project` lists its files with the same path and hash shape. `review_record` names a saved verdict where present. For avatar work, also retain `avatar_job`, the saved generation inputs, ids and answers; `avatar_evidence`, a list of relative `path`/`sha256` objects for every upload, padded WAV, receipt, completion marker, original download and conform/evidence bundle file; and, for a window, `base_segment` and `composite_receipt`. An avatar's decoded frame hashes come from the conform receipt, and a window's from the composite receipt, retained as `frame_hashes`. A prior generation may be referenced only after its Generation inputs match and its file identities pass. Missing files have no entry; never substitute an earlier key's output.
  - `window`, only where requested: `from`, `to`, `look`, `start_frame: s` (its first visible frame) and `end_frame: e` (the exclusive end, last visible frame `e - 1`), derived by the window pass. These are scene-local frame numbers; the caller adds the assembled scene start for its privacy frame. Earlier entries without this property read as before.
  - `duration` and `frames`: numbers from the timing output, on the scene's own entry beside `outputs`, or `null` before it returns.
  - `state`: `ready`, `unreviewed`, `unfinished` or `stopped`. `unreviewed` has `owed`, an array naming the Animator review or unresolved findings; `unfinished` has `owed` from the explainer's record; `stopped` has `reason`. A ready scene has no residue.
  - `review`: `verdict`, `ship`, `return` or `not_run`; and `key`, the key actually read, or `null` for `not_run`. Findings live in the saved review record and are named in `owed` while unresolved. A ready scene requires `verdict: "ship"` with `key` equal to the scene's key.

Hashes are 64 lowercase hexadecimal characters. All numbers are JSON numbers. Duplicate keys, invalid states, missing required fields or a ready scene with a mismatched review key stop the run with the malformed field named; leave that record unchanged. Read the record before changing it. Does it differ from the last read or this run's last successful write? Yes: stop and report the conflict without overwriting. No: save the changed scene and read the record back. Keep other scenes' entries and files unchanged; `order` comes only from the supplied list.

### 3. Timing, for every scene made

Avatar timing already prepared or retained in step 2 is reused here, with no second timing command. Otherwise prepare it as follows.

Write `timing-spec.json` in the scene folder with `frame`, `take.record`, the production's lead and tail, `captions` with `lines` only, and `marks`. Give each cue a unique mark name based on its table row, with `at: {"word": <exact At token>, "occurrence": <its occurrence>}`. Do not sort cues by time or change their words to make them fit.

For a window, resolve its exact `from` and `to` tokens and occurrences in the verified current take, with `a` the from word's recorded start and `b` the to word's recorded end. A missing token, empty span or span outside that take stops this window for corrected boundaries. Add `avatar-window-start` with `at: {"seconds": <lead plus the from word's recorded start minus 0.1>}` so the window shows from 0.1 seconds before its first word and that word's b, p or m closure falls inside `[s,e)`, and `avatar-window-end` with `at: {"seconds": <lead plus the to word's recorded end>}`, preserving the exact input number lexemes when adding seconds. Timing resolves their `frame` values as `s` and `e`; no frame arithmetic from seconds belongs here.

For avatar evidence, select up to six take words beginning with b, p or m (case-insensitive after edge punctuation), spread from start to end of the spoken part, all when fewer than six; for a window select only words in its span. Give each selected word a unique `avatar-word-<take index>` mark at its exact token and occurrence, retaining that association for step 6. Does that span have no eligible b, p or m word? Yes: select its first word for an onset sample instead, with the same named mark and word association. No: keep the selected bilabial samples. Add separately named marks at recorded silence boundaries inside the scene, including lead/tail boundaries; frame zero and timing's exclusive total frame count supply scene-edge boundaries without an out-of-scene mark. Keep these avatar/evidence marks separate from cue marks: only cue marks are explainer beats.

Build caption lines from the scene's narration after removing every complete square-bracket and angle-bracket tag, as `connectors/xai/CONNECTOR.md` in `wiser` counts words. Whitespace separates words; punctuation stays attached and a tag inside a word does not split it. A line is a sentence, which ends at a word whose last character, after any closing quotation mark or bracket, is `.`, `?` or `!`; a sentence over 84 Unicode code points splits at its last space at or before 84, repeating until it fits. Is there no space at which an overlong line can split? Yes: stop that scene and name the unsplittable word. No: keep the lines. Keep the words as said, with no written-form substitution. Each line's `at` names its first word and that exact token's occurrence in the whole scene, counted from one, never from the line.

From Scene Composer's directory run:

